## 🧮 How the Math Works: 2,049 Spinning Circles

Any closed 2D contour sampled at $N = 2049$ uniform arc-length points (centered on its exact geometric centroid so the $k = 0$ DC offset vanishes) can be represented as a discrete complex signal:

<div class="math-block">
$$
z_n = x_n + i\,y_n, \quad n \in \{0, 1, \dots, N - 1\}
$$
</div>

Using the **Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)** across symmetric frequencies $k \in [-1024, +1024]$, we compute each complex harmonic coefficient $X_k$:

<div class="math-block">
$$
X_k = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} z_n \, e^{-i \frac{2\pi}{N} k n} = A_k \, e^{i \varphi_k}
$$
</div>

where:
- $A_k = |X_k|$ is the **radius** of the $k$-th epicycle circle,
- $\varphi_k = \arg(X_k)$ is its **initial phase angle**, and
- $k$ is the **angular velocity** (in revolutions per cycle, spinning counterclockwise for $k > 0$ and clockwise for $k < 0$).

Sorting all $2049$ harmonics by descending amplitude ($A_{k_1} \ge A_{k_2} \ge \dots \ge A_{k_{2049}}$) allows us to reconstruct a partial contour $z_M(t)$ using only the top $M$ circles:

<div class="math-block">
$$
z_M(t) = \sum_{m=1}^{M} X_{k_m} \, e^{i \, 2\pi k_m t}, \quad t \in [0, 1)
$$
</div>

By **Parseval's Theorem**, the relative $L_2$ reconstruction error at harmonic truncation $M$ is simply the energy fraction residing in the unrendered tail harmonics $m > M$:

<div class="math-block">
$$
\text{Error}(M) = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{m=M+1}^{2049} |X_{k_m}|^2}{\sum_{m=1}^{2049} |X_{k_m}|^2}} \times 100\%
$$
</div>

## 💡 Pro-Tip: Why Fourier Epicycle Videos Look Pixelated on YouTube (and How to Fix It)

When I first uploaded the 1080p render to YouTube yesterday, the video looked surprisingly **pixelated and blocky** (*sgranatissimo*). Why does that happen?

1. **YouTube's 1080p Bitrate Starvation on Thin Vector Lines**: Glowing `1.7px` cyan and white vector circles moving at 60fps across a deep `#07080c` black background have extreme high-frequency spatial gradients. YouTube's standard `1080p60` transcode crushes video down to ~2.5–3.5 Mbps using 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, creating mosquito noise and macroblocking around thin moving lines.
2. **Fix #1 — Native HTML5 `<video>` Hosting on `ricc.rocks` (`-tune animation`)**: Because `ricc.rocks` serves static assets directly over Netlify CDN without re-transcoding, embedding a `<video>` MP4 encoded with `libx264 -crf 19 -tune animation -movflags +faststart` preserves **100% razor-sharp 1080p60 vector edges AND the 192 kbps stereo soundtrack** in just `16.8 MB` (whereas an equivalent 1080p60 `.gif` would have **no audio**, only 256 colors, and weigh over `80 MB`!).
3. **Fix #2 — Upscale to 4K (`3840×2160`) Before Uploading to YouTube**: If you upload to YouTube, upscale the 1080p master to `3840×2160` (`-vf "scale=3840:2160:flags=lanczos" -crf 17 -tune animation`). Uploading a 4K60 file forces YouTube to allocate its **`2160p60` VP9 / AV1 profile (~25–35 Mbps)**—nearly **10× the bitrate** of 1080p!
4. **Fix #3 — Run the Math Live in an HTML5 `<canvas>`**: Better yet, why stream pixels when you can ship the 2,049 complex numbers (`fourier_2049_coeffs.json`, just `48 KB`) and let the browser's GPU render the epicycles at native Retina/4K resolution?
